author: "Jason Huang"
summary: "This essay discusses how the Shing Mun Redoubt, the key position in Hong Kong's mainland defences, fell to a single Japanese battalion overnight on 9–10 December 1941. It argues that the British were probably going to lose the Redoubt anyway, but that the speed of the collapse came from avoidable failures: too few men, poorly used intelligence, breakdowns in communication, and procedural mistakes like the misplaced gate key. The Japanese, meanwhile, won through improvised risk-taking that was itself uncertain and nearly reversed by their own commanders."
